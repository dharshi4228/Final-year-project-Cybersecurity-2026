## Problem Statement
- Critical infrastructure systems are prime targets for Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs) that are skilled at bypassing traditional, signature-based defenses.  
- Once inside, attackers use stealthy methods like lateral movement, privilege escalation, and data theft that closely resemble normal user activity.  
- This creates a dangerous detection gap, giving attackers long dwell times and raising the risk of breaches or service disruptions.  
- Security teams are further burdened by too many low-quality alerts, making it hard to separate real threats from noise.  
- This project addresses the issue by designing a deception-based defense framework that generates reliable, context-rich alerts to quickly identify and stop malicious activity.  

## Background
Our motivation is to address the challenge of detecting stealthy, long-term Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs) that often bypass traditional defenses. Instead of relying on simple honeypots, we adopted a richer deception platform capable of mimicking realistic enterprise assets such as credentials, file shares, and Active Directory objects.  

This controlled deception environment allows us to safely observe attacker behavior, including tactics like lateral movement, privilege escalation, and data exfiltration. By aggregating deception alerts in Splunk and mapping them to the MITRE ATT&CK framework, we gain real-time visibility into adversary actions while validating our detection coverage.  

The significance of this work lies in providing a behavior-based defense model. Beyond academic contribution, this approach offers enterprises a practical and actionable strategy to enhance their security posture by making attackers expose themselves in a controlled, monitored setting.  

## Abstract
Critical infrastructure is a top target for Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs), which are hard to detect because they often look like normal network activity. Traditional security tools struggle to catch them, leading to delayed responses and higher risks.  

In this project, we use deception technology—such as decoys, traps, and fake assets—to lure attackers and expose their actions. These interactions produce clear and trustworthy alerts, helping security teams detect real threats faster, reduce false positives, and understand attacker behavior.  

This approach improves resilience and strengthens defense against advanced cyberattacks on critical infrastructure.
